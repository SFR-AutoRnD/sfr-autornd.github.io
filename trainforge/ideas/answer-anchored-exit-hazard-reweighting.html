Combined Answer Anchor + Exit Hazard

Apply both interventions together: double the total learning weight on verified final-answer and terminal-EOS labels, while adding a bounded penalty for premature EOS probability before the answer.

01 · Diagnosis

The observed failure signal

The frozen Qwen3-4B target diagnostic scored 138/300 overall: 105/240 on Omni-MATH, 15/30 on AIME 2025, and 18/30 on AIME 2026.

Sixty-two responses reached the 32K cap. Only 4/62 cap-hit responses were correct, compared with 134/238 non-cap responses. This 49.85-point association motivated the factorial test, but the contract explicitly treats it as correlation—not proof that truncation caused the errors.

02 · Causal hypothesis

What the experiment changes

If answer anchoring and premature-exit suppression address complementary failure modes, their combination should improve hard-problem accuracy or reduce 32K cap hits more than ordinary SFT on exactly the same examples.

03 · Training recipe

One data schedule, two localized learning signals.

The combined arm changes only the preregistered loss scales.

01

Freeze the hard-data schedule

Use 160 distinct Level 4–5 Hendrycks-MATH rows from 2,593 eligible examples, balanced across 14 category-by-level strata. Exclude the full diagnostic pool and target firewall.

02

Derive two masks

Locate the verified final-answer span and terminal EOS, then choose at most 32 deterministic positions before the answer. Missing or ambiguous boundaries fail closed.

03

Compose the objective

Apply normalized total weight 2.0 to the answer and terminal-stop labels, then add 0.1 × mean p(EOS) across the selected pre-answer positions.

04

Update the adapter

Run 20 AdamW updates with eight problems per update, bf16 LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, dropout 0, learning rate 1e-4, and gradient clipping at 1.

04 · Causal controls

What must stay fixed

  • Same initial Qwen3-4B parameter fingerprint for all four arms
  • Same 160 rows, order, tokenizer output, optimizer, seed, and checkpoint cadence
  • Every arm computes every loss component; only the two preregistered scales change
  • Evaluation rows and labels are unavailable to training
  • Every intervention is compared against the matched control on identical rows

05 · Ablations

What could explain the effect

  • Control: ordinary token-mean response SFT
  • Anchor-only: final-answer and terminal-EOS labels receive total weight 2.0
  • Hazard-only: add 0.1 × mean p(EOS) over at most 32 pre-answer positions
  • Combined: enable both mechanisms, exposing interaction rather than conflating them

06 · Deployment behavior

Training carries the full intervention.

  • No Python, calculator, verifier, search, or retrieval is added
  • No forced minimum length or modified EOS decoding
  • The adapter reloads on the unchanged Qwen3-4B base
  • The two learned loss effects can be ablated independently

Novelty status

Incremental method; novel candidates are separate.

This factorial combination is a controlled training method, not a verified novelty claim. Independently screened novel methods will be added as separate notebooks.

07 · Failure modes

How this idea could be wrong

  • The cap-hit association may reflect harder problems rather than harmful delayed publication.
  • Extra EOS pressure could shorten reasoning before the model reaches a correct answer.
  • One training seed cannot establish robustness.
  • Without anchor-only and hazard-only arms, a positive result would not isolate which component mattered.
