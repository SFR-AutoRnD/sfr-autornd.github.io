Premature-Exit Hazard Suppression

Penalize the probability of emitting EOS at a small deterministic set of positions before the verified final answer, while leaving ordinary response-token SFT otherwise unchanged.

Training methodTeach the model where it must not stop yet.
Signal
Pre-answer EOS probability
Penalty
0.1 × bounded mean hazard
Inference
No forced minimum length

01 · Diagnosis

Long reasoning frequently terminates at the response boundary.

The frozen diagnostic produced 62 cap hits among 300 hard math responses. Cap-hit accuracy was 4/62, while non-cap accuracy was 134/238. That gap motivates a test of termination behavior, without asserting that every capped response contained a recoverable answer.

02 · Causal hypothesis

Suppress only demonstrably premature stopping.

A small penalty on EOS probability before the verified answer may preserve the reasoning trajectory long enough to reach a correct conclusion, without globally rewarding longer generations.

03 · Training recipe

A bounded penalty at deterministic pre-answer positions.

The objective changes stopping pressure during training without imposing a decoding rule later.

01

Build one matched schedule

Use 160 distinct Level 4–5 MATH rows sampled from 2,593 eligible examples and balanced across 14 category-by-level strata. Exclude all diagnostic and held-out rows.

02

Find valid pre-answer sites

Locate the verified answer boundary, then choose at most 32 deterministic token positions before it. Invalid or missing boundaries are rejected instead of approximated.

03

Compute the hazard addend

At each selected position read p(EOS), average those probabilities, and add 0.1 times that bounded mean to ordinary response-token cross entropy.

04

Update the adapter

Run 20 AdamW updates, eight problems per update, bf16 LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, dropout 0, learning rate 1e-4, and gradient clipping at 1. Answer anchoring stays off.

04 · Matched controls

The stopping loss is the only moving part.

  • Initial weights, data rows, order, tokens, seed, and optimizer stay fixed
  • Answer-anchor scale remains zero
  • Every arm computes the same objective components
  • The added loss is mathematically bounded to [0, 0.1]
  • Checkpoint and adapter identities are hash-bound

05 · Deployment behavior

The model chooses when to stop.

  • No minimum response length is imposed
  • No EOS token is masked during decoding
  • No Python, calculator, verifier, retrieval, or search is added
  • The learned distribution is the entire intervention

06 · Failure modes

How this idea could fail

  • The cap may mark intrinsically difficult examples rather than a stopping-policy defect.
  • EOS suppression can prolong unproductive loops and increase cap hits.
  • Thirty-two sampled positions may miss the decision boundary that matters.
  • One training seed cannot establish robustness or generality.
