Qwen3-4B · hard-math training

Proof-Diamond Commutation Training

Equivalent proof orders should close to the same internal state.

01 · Thesis

A proof can branch without changing what is known.

Some proof steps depend on one another; reversing them is invalid. Other steps operate on independent facts, so applying step a then b or b then a reaches the same mathematical state. A linear reference solution exposes only one of those legal paths.

When two proof steps genuinely commute, train both sequences to close at the same representation—and contrast them with a nearby order that violates a real dependency.

The method uses proof structure as supervision without requiring a proof graph or verifier during deployment.

02 · Exact novelty delta

Certified commuting squares become the learning unit.

Prior work permutes order-insensitive predicates, models reasoning DAGs, or rewards diverse complete trajectories. The proposed delta combines three constraints in a decoder-only informal-math model: verifier-certified commuting proof diamonds, representation closure after the two legal paths, and a matched invalid-edge contrast.

The 8.4 score is an internal hypothesis screen, with lower confidence because natural-language proofs may hide dependencies. It must not be described as verified novelty before external review.

03 · Real-data construction

Find diamonds before allocating a training run.

The admission audit is part of the method: insufficient or ambiguous diamonds stop the experiment.

01

Freeze candidate proofs

Search a category-balanced set of at most 160 Hendrycks-MATH Level 4–5 training solutions. The compute-matched baseline uses exactly the same selected rows.

02

Infer dependencies

Segment atomic steps and estimate symbol read/write sets. Propose a pair only when neither step consumes a fact introduced exclusively by the other.

03

Replay both paths

Symbolically apply a then b and b then a. Admit the pair only if both paths are legal and terminate in the same normalized state.

04

Audit coverage

Require at least 120 certified diamonds from 80 problems and manually inspect 30 with zero dependency or semantic errors. Otherwise, do not train.

04 · Objective

Two valid orders, one closure state.

Use ordinary cross entropy on both legal linearizations. Add Lclosure = 1 − cos(hab, hba) after the diamond closes, plus a local ranking loss preferring a legal ordering over one verifier-certified dependency violation.

Start with closure weight 0.05 and ranking weight 0.1 so the base language objective remains dominant.

05 · Training contract

Make the structural loss auditable.

  • Qwen3-4B LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, dropout 0, bf16
  • 20 AdamW updates; batches of four problems containing both legal paths
  • Same rows, order, seed, tokens, and forward-pass count in control
  • Segmentation, graph, replay, adapter, and checkpoint identities hash-bound
  • 32K response budget inside a 40,960-token context

06 · Deployment

Return to ordinary generation.

  • Reload the adapter on the unchanged Qwen3-4B base
  • Generate one normal solution with standard decoding
  • Add no Python, search, retrieval, calculator, proof graph, or verifier
  • Do not expose hidden-state or dependency labels to the model

08 · Failure modes

The most distinctive idea also has the hardest admission gate.

  • Natural-language steps may depend on unstated facts that symbol read/write sets miss.
  • True commuting diamonds may be too sparse in hard proofs to support training.
  • Forcing hidden-state agreement can erase useful path-specific information.
  • The invalid ordering may contain superficial cues that make ranking too easy.
  • External review may find an equivalent commutation loss and lower the novelty assessment.
