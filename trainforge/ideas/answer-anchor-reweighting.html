Answer-Anchor Reweighting

Teach the model to protect the verified final answer and terminal stop by assigning those labels twice the normalized learning weight, without adding an inference-time tool or changing the training examples.

Training methodProtect the verified answer tokens inside a long solution.
Supervision
Final-answer span + terminal stop
Objective change
Normalized total weight 1.0 → 2.0
Inference
No new tools or decoding rule

01 · Diagnosis

Correct answers are often never published.

On the frozen 300-problem target diagnostic, Qwen3-4B answered 138 correctly. Sixty-two generations reached the 32K response limit; only 4/62 cap-hit responses were correct, versus 134/238 responses that stopped before the cap.

This is a strong association, not a causal conclusion. The answer-anchor arm tests one narrow explanation: the ordinary token-average objective may underweight the few tokens that serialize the final answer after a long derivation.

02 · Causal hypothesis

Protect the answer without forcing an early stop.

Upweighting only verified final-answer and terminal-EOS labels should improve exact-answer publication while preserving the model’s freedom to reason for as long as the problem requires.

03 · Training recipe

Four steps from hard problem to weighted update.

The intervention changes only the loss assigned to a verified answer boundary.

01

Build the hard-data schedule

Sample 160 distinct Hendrycks-MATH Level 4–5 rows from 2,593 tokenizer-eligible examples. Balance 14 category-by-level strata at 11–12 rows each and exclude every diagnostic or target row.

02

Locate the answer boundary

Tokenize the complete worked solution, then derive a deterministic mask for the verified final-answer span and terminal EOS. If the boundary cannot be located, fail closed instead of guessing.

03

Normalize anchor weight

Keep ordinary response-token cross entropy, but rescale the answer-span and terminal-stop labels so their normalized total contribution is 2.0. All other response labels retain ordinary weight.

04

Update a matched adapter

Run 20 AdamW updates with eight problems per update, bf16 LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, dropout 0, learning rate 1e-4, and gradient clipping at 1. The exit-hazard scale remains zero.

04 · Matched controls

Only one training knob moves.

  • Initial model fingerprint, 160 rows, order, tokens, seed, and optimizer stay identical
  • Every arm computes every loss component
  • Only the answer-anchor scale changes from 0 to 1
  • Held-out labels remain unavailable to training
  • Checkpoint and adapter identities are hash-bound

05 · Deployment behavior

No inference-time scaffolding.

  • The adapter reloads on the unchanged Qwen3-4B base
  • No Python, search, retrieval, calculator, or verifier is added
  • No forced early stop or answer extractor changes generation
  • The learned token distribution is the entire intervention

06 · Failure modes

How this idea could fail

  • The 32K cap may merely identify harder problems, so anchor weighting may not improve reasoning.
  • Extra weight on a wrong or poorly delimited answer span can amplify label noise.
  • The model may learn terse answer publication without improving the mathematical derivation.
  • One training seed and 90 held-out problems estimate direction, not robustness.
