Qwen3-4B · hard-math training

Verifier-Quotiented Equation Orbit Likelihood

Train on the equation, not its typography.

01 · Thesis

One mathematical state has many correct strings.

A reference solution commits to one arbitrary rendering of an equation. Factorization, expansion, fraction combination, equality-side swaps, sign normalization, and commutative reorderings can express the same mathematical state, yet ordinary cross entropy treats every unchosen form as lost probability mass.

For a certified equation window, supervise the equivalence class as one target instead of forcing the model to imitate one typography.

Prose, connective reasoning, and the final answer remain under ordinary token-level supervision. Only a narrow, symbolically certified equation span becomes set-valued.

02 · Exact novelty delta

Likelihood on a quotient class inside a free-form solution.

Diverse-trajectory fine-tuning learns from multiple correct samples, and robustness work evaluates equivalent formulations. The proposed delta is to define a single supervised object as a quotient class under certified algebraic rewrites and marginalize its likelihood inside an otherwise autoregressive hard-math response.

The 8.2 score is an internal screen of that precise mechanism. It remains a hypothesis until external reviewers confirm that the same objective has not already been published.

03 · Real-data construction

Build small certified orbits, then fail closed.

Training uses real Hendrycks-MATH Level 4–5 solutions only; Omni-MATH and AIME remain outside construction and selection.

01

Freeze the rows

Select 160 unique problems, stratified by category and level, before transformation. Bind the exact row IDs and exclude every diagnostic and target item.

02

Choose one window

Parse one equation span per problem. Reject ambiguous LaTeX, implicit-domain expressions, and windows that cannot round-trip through the symbolic representation.

03

Generate the orbit

Create two to four equivalents using bounded factor/expand, fraction, equality-side, sign, or commutative rewrites. Keep alternatives within a narrow token-length band.

04

Certify coverage

Require exact equivalence and declared-domain checks, at least 160 spans from 80 problems, and a 30-span manual audit with zero semantic errors.

04 · Objective

Sum probability before taking the log.

Lorbit = −log((1/K) Σj Pθ(y(j) | x))

Here y(j) is one certified rendering in an orbit of size K. The loss rewards total probability assigned to the mathematical equivalence class; ordinary cross entropy remains on prose and final-answer tokens.

05 · Training contract

Match examples and actual compute.

  • Qwen3-4B LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, dropout 0, bf16
  • AdamW near 1e−4 for 20–30 real optimizer updates
  • Canonical control uses identical rows, order, seed, and forward-pass count
  • Transform set, parser, data, model, and adapter identities are hash-bound
  • 32K response budget inside a 40,960-token context

06 · Deployment

No quotient machinery at inference.

  • Reload the adapter on the unchanged Qwen3-4B base
  • Generate one normal text solution with standard decoding
  • Add no Python, symbolic algebra, search, retrieval, or verifier
  • Keep the existing answer parser and task interface

07 · Closest work

  • Representation Robustness Under Executable Reasoning Constraints tests equivalent formulations as perturbations
  • MathBridge addresses language–LaTeX translation ambiguity

08 · Failure modes

Every rewrite is a possible source of leakage or noise.

  • A parser can silently change scope, precedence, or the meaning of a LaTeX expression.
  • Algebraic equivalence may fail under hidden domain assumptions.
  • Marginalization can prefer shorter renderings for tokenization rather than mathematical reasons.
  • Certified coverage may be too sparse to justify training.
  • External review may identify an equivalent set-likelihood method and lower the novelty assessment.
